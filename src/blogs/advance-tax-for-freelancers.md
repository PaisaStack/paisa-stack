  answer: 1% per month on the shortfall. <div class="callout good" id="short-answer"><b>Short answer:</b> If you're a freelancer using the <b>44ADA presumptive scheme</b>, you don't have to pay anything on 15 December. You can pay <b>100% of your advance tax in one go by 15 March 2027</b>. If you keep regular books and declare actual profit, you must have paid <b>75% of the year's tax by 15 December 2026</b>. Either way, advance tax applies only if your tax after TDS is <b>₹10,000 or more</b>. Miss a date and you pay 1% interest per month on the shortfall.</div>

<p>Most advance tax articles tell every freelancer to pay 75% by 15 December. That's true for some of you, but if you're on the presumptive scheme, it isn't. Paying early doesn't hurt, but paying in a panic before you know your real number often does.</p>
<p>This guide covers Tax Year 2026-27, the first year under the new Income-tax Act 2025. It shows who actually owes what on 15 December and what missing it costs, with four freelancer examples in rupees.</p>
<div class="callout"><b>New section numbers:</b> From 1 April 2026, the presumptive scheme for professionals (old 44ADA) sits in Section 58 of the Income-tax Act 2025. Advance tax due dates are in Section 408, and interest (old 234B and 234C) is now Sections 424 and 425. Rates and dates stayed the same. We use "44ADA" in this guide because that's the name everyone searches for.</div>

<h2 id="who-pays">Do you need to pay advance tax at all?</h2>
<p>You must pay advance tax if your <b>estimated tax for the year, minus TDS and TCS, is ₹10,000 or more</b>. That's it. There's no separate rule for freelancers, salaried people or traders.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Income up to ₹12 lakh:</b> in the new regime, the rebate makes your tax zero, so there's nothing to pay in advance. Under 44ADA, only 50% of receipts is treated as income, so this covers freelancers with up to <b>₹24 lakh of receipts</b>.</li>
<li><b>Clients deduct TDS:</b> Indian clients usually deduct 10% TDS on professional fees. If that covers your tax, you owe no advance tax. Foreign clients and many platforms deduct nothing.</li>
<li><b>Senior citizens:</b> residents aged 60 or above are exempt, but only if they have no business or professional income. A retired consultant who still bills clients is not exempt.</li>
</ul>

<p>"Cumulative" means the total paid by that date. If you paid 45% by September, you pay another 30% by 15 December, not 75% again.</p>

<h2 id="44ada-rule">The 44ADA one-instalment rule</h2>
<p>44ADA lets specified professionals such as doctors, lawyers, CAs, architects, engineers and technical consultants declare <b>50% of gross receipts as income</b> without keeping full books. For Tax Year 2026-27 the limit is <b>₹50 lakh of receipts</b>, or <b>₹75 lakh</b> if cash receipts are within 5% of the total.</p>
<p>If you use it, the law asks for just one advance tax payment: the <b>whole amount by 15 March 2027</b>. No June, September or December instalment is required, and no interest is charged for skipping them. This is the single biggest relief for freelancers, and most people don't know they have it.</p>
<p>The catch is that you must actually declare income under 44ADA when you file. If you later switch to declaring actual profit, the regular four-instalment schedule applies and interest for the missed instalments comes back.</p>

<h2 id="examples">4 worked examples (new regime, Tax Year 2026-27)</h2>
<p>All examples use the new regime slabs (0 to ₹4 lakh nil, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% in ₹4 lakh steps), the rebate up to ₹12 lakh, and 4% cess. Freelance income doesn't get the ₹75,000 standard deduction; that's only for salary and pension.</p>

<h3>Example 1: Riya, designer, ₹20 lakh receipts, 44ADA</h3>
<p>Presumptive income is 50% of ₹20 lakh = <b>₹10,00,000</b>. That's below ₹12 lakh, so the rebate makes her tax <b>zero</b>. She owes no advance tax at all. If her Indian clients deducted TDS, she gets it all back as a refund when she files.</p>

<h3>Example 2: Arjun, developer, ₹30 lakh from foreign clients, 44ADA</h3>
<p>Presumptive income is <b>₹15,00,000</b>. Tax: ₹20,000 (4 to 8 lakh) + ₹40,000 (8 to 12 lakh) + ₹45,000 (15% of 3 lakh) = ₹1,05,000, plus cess = <b>₹1,09,200</b>. His foreign clients deduct no TDS.</p>
<table>
<tr><th>When Arjun pays</th><th>Interest</th></tr>
<tr><td>Full ₹1,09,200 by 15 March 2027</td><td>₹0</td></tr>
<tr><td>Full amount on 31 March 2027</td><td>₹1,092 (1% for March)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Nothing until filing his ITR in July 2027</td><td>₹1,092 + ₹4,368 (4 months at 1%) = <b>₹5,460</b></td></tr>
</table>
<p>Nothing is due on 15 December. He can park the money in a liquid fund or sweep FD until March and earn a little on it.</p>

<h3>Example 3: Meera, consultant, regular books, ₹30 lakh profit</h3>
<p>Meera's receipts are above the 44ADA limit, so she keeps books and declares actual profit of <b>₹30,00,000</b>. Tax: ₹3,00,000 up to ₹24 lakh + ₹1,80,000 (30% of ₹6 lakh) = ₹4,80,000, plus cess = <b>₹4,99,200</b>.</p>
<table>
<tr><th>Due date</th><th>Cumulative %</th><th>Total paid by then</th><th>This instalment</th></tr>
<tr><td>15 June 2026</td><td>15%</td><td>₹74,880</td><td>₹74,880</td></tr>
<tr><td>15 September 2026</td><td>45%</td><td>₹2,24,640</td><td>₹1,49,760</td></tr>
<tr><td>15 December 2026</td><td>75%</td><td>₹3,74,400</td><td>₹1,49,760</td></tr>
<tr><td>15 March 2027</td><td>100%</td><td>₹4,99,200</td><td>₹1,24,800</td></tr>
</table>
<p>If Meera skipped June and September and pays the full ₹3,74,400 on 15 December, she owes Section 425 interest of 3% on the June shortfall (about ₹2,244) and 3% on the September shortfall (about ₹6,738). That's roughly <b>₹8,982</b>, but no further interest if she pays the rest by 15 March.</p>

<h3>Example 4: Karan, salaried, with ₹8 lakh side freelance receipts</h3>
<p>Karan's ₹12 lakh CTC gives a taxable salary of ₹10,24,140 (see the <a href="../calculators/salary-in-hand-calculator.html">salary in-hand calculator</a>), so his employer deducts no TDS. His side work brings ₹8 lakh of receipts, and under 44ADA, ₹4,00,000 of that is income. Total taxable income: <b>₹14,24,140</b>. Tax with cess: <b>₹97,366</b>.</p>
<p>All of it is due from him, because his employer's TDS is zero. He has two options: pay it as advance tax by 15 March 2027, or tell his HR about the side income so they deduct extra TDS from his remaining salary months. Many people in Karan's position get a surprise bill in July because they assumed their employer was handling tax.</p>

<h2 id="interest">What paying late costs (interest)</h2>
<table>
<tr><th>New section (old)</th><th>When it applies</th><th>Rate</th></tr>
<tr><td>Section 425 (234C)</td><td>Shortfall on an instalment date</td><td>1% per month: 3 months for June, September and December shortfalls, 1 month for March</td></tr>
<tr><td>Section 424 (234B)</td><td>Less than 90% of tax paid by 31 March</td><td>1% per month from 1 April until you pay</td></tr>
</table>
<p>Interest is simple, not compound, and any part of a month counts as a full month. It's paid along with your self-assessment tax before filing. Regular taxpayers get a small cushion: no Section 425 interest if at least 12% is paid by 15 June and 36% by 15 September.</p>
<p>Interest is annoying but rarely huge. The bigger risk is not paying at all and then filing late. That adds a late fee and more interest, which our <a href="belated-itr-last-date-penalty.html">belated ITR guide</a> covers.</p>

<h2 id="how-much">How to work out your 15 December amount</h2>
<ol>
<li><b>Estimate full-year receipts.</b> Take what you've invoiced from April to November and add a realistic figure for December to March. Count signed contracts, not hopes.</li>
<li><b>Work out income.</b> On 44ADA, it's 50% of receipts. On regular books, it's receipts minus actual expenses.</li>
<li><b>Add other income.</b> This means salary, FD interest, rent and capital gains.</li>
<li><b>Calculate tax.</b> Use the <a href="../calculators/income-tax-calculator.html">Income Tax calculator</a> on your total income, and the <a href="../calculators/freelance-tax-44ada-calculator.html">44ADA calculator</a> for the freelance part.</li>
<li><b>Subtract TDS.</b> Check Form 26AS (now Form 168) or the AIS on the income tax portal for TDS your clients have already deducted.</li>
<li><b>Apply the percentage.</b> On 44ADA, nothing is due until 15 March. On regular books, 75% of the net figure, minus what you've already paid, is due by 15 December.</li>
</ol>
<p>If your income is lumpy, it's better to slightly overpay than underpay. Any excess comes back as a refund with interest after you file.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-pay">How to pay advance tax online, step by step</h2>
<ol>
<li>Go to <b>incometax.gov.in</b> and log in, or use "e-Pay Tax" without logging in using your PAN and mobile OTP.</li>
<li>Choose <b>e-Pay Tax → New Payment</b> and select the Income Tax tile.</li>
<li>Pick <b>Tax Year 2026-27</b> and the type of payment <b>"Advance Tax" (minor head 100)</b>. Picking the wrong head is the most common mistake.</li>
<li>Enter the amount, choose net banking, UPI, debit card or a bank counter, and pay.</li>
<li>Download the challan receipt. Note the BSR code and challan number; you'll enter them in your ITR.</li>
</ol>
<p>Payment usually shows up in your Form 26AS within a few days. Don't wait until the evening of 15 December; bank and portal slowdowns on deadline day are common.</p>

<h2 id="mistakes">Mistakes freelancers make with advance tax</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>Assuming TDS covers everything.</b> On 44ADA, 10% TDS from Indian clients often covers your tax. But foreign clients and many platforms deduct nothing, and on regular books with high profit your real rate can be well above 10%.</li>
<li><b>Paying 75% in December when on 44ADA.</b> It's not wrong, but you didn't have to, and that money could have earned interest until March.</li>
<li><b>Claiming the ₹75,000 standard deduction on freelance income.</b> It only applies to salary and pension.</li>
<li><b>Ignoring GST.</b> Advance tax is income tax only. If your receipts cross ₹20 lakh, GST registration is a separate obligation. See our guide to <a href="freelancing-gst-invoicing.html">GST and invoicing for freelancers</a>.</li>
<li><b>Wrong minor head.</b> Paying as "Self-Assessment Tax (300)" instead of "Advance Tax (100)" before 31 March can lead to interest notices. Fix it with challan correction on the portal.</li>
</ul>

<div class="callout"><b>How we calculated:</b> New regime slabs for Tax Year 2026-27 (unchanged from FY 2025-26), rebate up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income, 4% cess, and 44ADA presumptive income at 50% of gross receipts. Interest figures are simple 1% per month on the shortfall rounded down to the nearest ₹100. Your exact interest may differ by a few rupees.</div>
