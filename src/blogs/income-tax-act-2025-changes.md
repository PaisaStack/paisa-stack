faqs:
- question: When did the Income-tax Act, 2025 come into force?
  answer: On 1 April 2026. It replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 for income earned from FY 2026-27 (Tax Year 2026-27) onwards. Income of FY 2025-26 and earlier years is still governed by the 1961 Act.
- question: What is the new section number for 80C?
  answer: Section 123, read with Schedule XV. The limit is unchanged at ₹1,50,000, and it still applies only under the old tax regime. NPS 80CCD(1B) is now Section 124, and 80D health insurance is Section 126.
- question: What is a tax year under the new Act?
  answer: A tax year is the 12-month financial year in which you earn the income, from 1 April to 31 March. It replaces both previous year and assessment year. Income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 is Tax Year 2026-27, and its return is due in 2027.
- question: Did tax slabs or rates change with the new Act?
  answer: No. The new regime slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the full rebate up to ₹12 lakh taxable income are the same for Tax Year 2026-27 as they were for FY 2025-26.
- question: Which law applies to my ITR due on 31 July 2026?
  answer: The 1961 Act. That return is for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), so it uses the old section numbers and old ITR forms. If you missed the deadline, a belated return can still be filed until 31 December 2026.
- question: What is Form 16 called now?
  answer: For salary paid from April 2026, the TDS certificate is Form 130 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Form 26AS becomes Form 168 and Form 12BB becomes Form 124. Certificates for FY 2025-26 salary still use the old names.
- question: Do I lose my old capital loss carried forward?
  answer: No. Losses determined under the 1961 Act can be carried forward and set off under the new Act, for the remaining years they were eligible, as long as the loss return was filed on time.
- question: Naya Income Tax Act 2025 se mera tax badhega kya?
  answer: Nahi. Rates, slabs aur deduction limits same hain. Sirf section aur form ke numbers badle hain, aur assessment year ki jagah tax year aa gaya hai.

<div class="callout good" id="short-answer"><b>Short answer:</b> The <b>Income-tax Act, 2025</b> replaced the 1961 Act from <b>1 April 2026</b>. Your tax rates, slabs and deduction limits did <b>not</b> change. What changed is the language and numbering: "assessment year" became <b>"tax year"</b>, 80C is now <b>Section 123</b>, 87A is <b>Section 156</b>, the new regime is <b>Section 202</b>, and Form 16 is <b>Form 130</b>. Income up to 31 March 2026 (FY 2025-26) is still taxed under the old Act.</div>

<p>If you've seen "Section 123" on an investment proof form or heard that "AY is gone", you're not alone in being confused. The government rewrote the entire income tax law in simpler language. It cut the Act from over 800 sections to 536, and from 47 chapters to 23, with 16 schedules. The aim was easier reading and fewer cross-references, not higher tax.</p>
<p>This guide explains what changed in plain words. It covers which Act applies to which year, a lookup table of the sections and forms you'll actually meet, and four everyday examples.</p>

<h2 id="which-act">Which Act applies to which year</h2>
<p>This is the single most important thing to get right, because both laws are in use right now.</p>
<table>
<tr><th>Your income earned in</th><th>Law that applies</th><th>Return called</th><th>Return due</th></tr>
<tr><td>FY 2025-26 (Apr 2025 – Mar 2026)</td><td>Income-tax Act, 1961</td><td>AY 2026-27</td><td>31 July 2026 (belated till 31 Dec 2026)</td></tr>
<tr><td>FY 2026-27 (Apr 2026 – Mar 2027)</td><td>Income-tax Act, 2025</td><td>Tax Year 2026-27</td><td>From July 2027</td></tr>
</table>
<p>So if you're still filing last year's return, you use the old section numbers and old ITR forms. If you missed 31 July, read our <a href="belated-itr-last-date-penalty.html">belated ITR guide</a>: the 31 December 2026 deadline is under the 1961 Act.</p>
<p>The same split applies to TDS. The official CBDT FAQs say the deciding date is the <b>earlier of payment or credit</b>. If that happened on or before 31 March 2026, the old Act applies. If it happened on or after 1 April 2026, the new Act applies.</p>

<h2 id="tax-year">"Tax year" in plain words</h2>
<p>Under the old law you earned income in the "previous year" and it was assessed in the "assessment year". Earning in 2025-26 and filing for "AY 2026-27" confused almost everyone.</p>
<p>The new Act uses one term. A <b>tax year</b> is the financial year in which you earn the income, from 1 April to 31 March. Income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 belongs to <b>Tax Year 2026-27</b>, and you file its return in 2027. One year, one label.</p>
<div class="callout"><b>Quick test:</b> When someone says "Tax Year 2026-27", think "the money I earn between April 2026 and March 2027". That's it.</div>

<h2 id="what-changed">What actually changed</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>Section numbers.</b> Almost every familiar section has a new number. Deductions moved from Chapter VI-A to a new chapter, and the 80-series became Sections 122 to 154.</li>
<li><b>Tax year</b> replaces previous year and assessment year.</li>
<li><b>TDS is consolidated.</b> Salary TDS is Section 392. Most other TDS (on interest, rent, property, professional fees and so on) sits in one table-based Section 393, instead of 194A, 194I, 194IA, 194J and dozens of others. TCS is Section 394.</li>
<li><b>All returns under one section.</b> Original, belated, revised and updated returns are all covered by Section 263.</li>
<li><b>Forms renumbered</b> under the new Income-tax Rules, 2026. Form 16, 26AS, 12BB and 15G/15H all have new numbers (table below).</li>
<li><b>Simpler language.</b> Long provisos and explanations were turned into tables and formulas, so the law reads more like a manual.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="examples">4 examples from real life</h2>

<h3>1. Riya, salaried, ₹11 lakh salary in Tax Year 2026-27</h3>
<p>Taxable income after the ₹75,000 standard deduction (now Section 19) is ₹10,25,000. That's under ₹12 lakh, so the rebate (now Section 156) makes her tax <b>₹0</b>, exactly as under the old Act. Next June her employer will give her <b>Form 130</b> instead of Form 16. Nothing else changes for her.</p>

<h3>2. Sharma ji, 68, with ₹4 lakh of FD interest</h3>
<p>Every April he submitted Form 15H to his bank so no TDS was cut. From Tax Year 2026-27 he submits <b>Form 121</b>, which replaces both 15G and 15H. The ₹50,000 senior citizen deduction on deposit interest (old 80TTB, now Section 153) still applies under the old regime. If he stays in the new regime, his taxable income of ₹4 lakh is below the ₹4 lakh nil slab anyway.</p>

<h3>3. Aman buys an ₹80 lakh flat in November 2026</h3>
<p>TDS on property worth ₹50 lakh or more is still <b>1%</b>, so he deducts ₹80,000 from the payment to the seller. The only change is the form: he files <b>Form 141</b> instead of Form 26QB, under Section 393 instead of 194IA. Our <a href="../calculators/tds-on-property-calculator.html">TDS on property calculator</a> works out the amount.</p>

<h3>4. Neha's ₹1,20,000 stock loss from FY 2025-26</h3>
<p>She booked a short-term capital loss in FY 2025-26 and filed her return on time, before 31 July 2026. The CBDT FAQs confirm that losses determined under the old Act <b>carry forward</b> into the new Act for their remaining years. So she can set the ₹1,20,000 off against her gains in Tax Year 2026-27. If she had filed late, the loss would have been lost under the old Act's own rules, as our <a href="belated-itr-last-date-penalty.html">belated return guide</a> explains.</p>

<h2 id="not-changed">What did not change</h2>
<ul>
<li>Tax slabs and rates in both regimes.</li>
<li>Standard deduction: ₹75,000 (new regime), ₹50,000 (old regime).</li>
<li>Zero tax up to ₹12 lakh taxable income under the new regime, which means a ₹12.75 lakh salary.</li>
<li>Deduction limits: ₹1.5 lakh under 123 (old 80C), ₹50,000 NPS, 80D limits, ₹2 lakh home loan interest.</li>
<li>Your freedom to choose between the old and new regime.</li>
<li>Interest rates on late payment of TDS.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="todo">What you should do now</h2>
<ol>
<li><b>Finish FY 2025-26 under the old Act.</b> If your return is pending, file it before 31 December 2026.</li>
<li><b>Update your investment proof language.</b> When HR asks for proofs in January–February 2027, the declaration is Form 124 and 80C is "Section 123". The amounts and documents are the same.</li>
<li><b>Seniors: submit Form 121</b> to your bank instead of 15G/15H for deposits that earn interest from April 2026.</li>
<li><b>Re-check your regime.</b> Since the slabs didn't change, last year's choice probably still holds. Run your numbers in the <a href="../calculators/income-tax-calculator.html">Income Tax calculator</a>, and use the <a href="../calculators/hra-exemption-calculator.html">HRA calculator</a> if you pay rent.</li>
<li><b>Don't panic at new numbers on documents.</b> A "Section 392" line on your payslip is just salary TDS.</li>
</ol>
