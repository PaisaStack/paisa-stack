<div class="callout good" id="short-answer"><b>Short answer:</b> A ₹10 lakh CTC gives you about <b>₹71,129 a month in hand</b> in Tax Year 2026-27, if your basic is 50% of CTC and PF is cut on your full basic. If your employer caps PF at the ₹25,000 wage ceiling, it is about <b>₹75,129</b>. Your <b>income tax is zero</b> under the new regime, because taxable income stays well below ₹12 lakh. The gap between CTC and in-hand is mostly PF (yours and your employer's) and gratuity, which is still your money, just not monthly money.</div>

<p>You got an offer for 10 LPA. You divide by 12, get ₹83,333, and start planning. Then the first salary slip shows something closer to ₹71,000 and you wonder where ₹12,000 went.</p>
<p>Nothing went missing. CTC is the total cost a company spends on you in a year, and some of that cost never reaches your bank account each month. This guide shows the exact breakup of a ₹10 lakh CTC with real numbers, explains why your tax is zero, and covers the two 2026 changes most salary pages still miss: the <b>50% wage rule</b> under the new labour codes and the <b>EPF wage ceiling rising to ₹25,000</b>.</p>

<h2 id="ctc-vs-in-hand">CTC vs in-hand, in plain words</h2>
<p>Think of CTC as three layers:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Money the company pays for you but not to you:</b> employer PF (12% of basic) and gratuity (about 4.81% of basic). These sit in your CTC but never show up in the monthly salary.</li>
<li><b>Gross salary:</b> what's left. This is the "earnings" total on your payslip: basic, HRA, special allowance and so on.</li>
<li><b>In-hand (net) salary:</b> gross minus your own deductions: employee PF (12% of basic), professional tax (about ₹200 a month in most states) and TDS (income tax).</li>
</ul>
<p>So the formula is simple: <b>In-hand = CTC − employer PF − gratuity − employee PF − professional tax − income tax</b>. Everything else in this article is just filling in those numbers.</p>

<p>Since the labour codes came into force, most companies are setting basic pay at 50% of CTC. Here is the full breakup with PF cut on the full basic, which is the most common setup for new offers.</p>

<p>Two things to notice. First, ₹1,20,000 a year goes into your PF account (₹60,000 from you, ₹60,000 from your employer), so you are saving ₹10,000 a month without trying. Second, the ₹24,050 gratuity provision is only paid out if you complete 5 years of service, so for most job-hoppers that part of CTC is never received.</p>
<div class="callout"><b>Professional tax depends on your state.</b> It is about ₹2,400 to ₹2,500 a year in states like Maharashtra and Karnataka (Maharashtra charges ₹300 in February instead of ₹200), and zero in states like Delhi, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh. If you live in a no-PT state, add ₹200 a month to every figure in this article.</div>

<h2 id="tax">Why your tax is zero at 10 LPA</h2>
<p>In Tax Year 2026-27 (FY 2026-27), the new regime is the default and its slabs are:</p>
<table>
<tr><th>Taxable income</th><th>Rate</th></tr>
<tr><td>Up to ₹4,00,000</td><td>Nil</td></tr>
<tr><td>₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000</td><td>5%</td></tr>
<tr><td>₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000</td><td>10%</td></tr>
<tr><td>₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000</td><td>15%</td></tr>
<tr><td>₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000</td><td>20%</td></tr>
<tr><td>₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000</td><td>25%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Above ₹24,00,000</td><td>30%</td></tr>
</table>
<p>Salaried people also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, and the rebate (Section 87A under the old Act, now Section 156 of the Income-tax Act 2025) wipes out the tax completely if taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less.</p>
<p>For our ₹10 lakh CTC: gross salary ₹9,15,950 − standard deduction ₹75,000 = <b>taxable income ₹8,40,950</b>. The slab tax on that would be ₹24,095, but it's below ₹12 lakh, so the rebate makes it <b>₹0</b>. No TDS should be cut from your salary at all. If your employer is deducting TDS at 10 LPA, check whether they've put you in the old regime by mistake.</p>
<p>Notice that employer PF and gratuity are removed before tax. Employer PF contributions up to 12% of basic are not taxed in your hands, and gratuity is not salary until it's paid. That's why your taxable income is lower than your CTC by much more than the standard deduction.</p>

<p>The same CTC can give very different monthly salaries. These four cases cover most offers we see. All use professional tax of ₹200 a month and the new regime.</p>

<h3>Example 1: Basic 50%, PF on full basic (most new offers)</h3>
<p>This is the table above. Employee and employer PF are each ₹5,000 a month. <b>In-hand: ₹71,129 a month.</b> Tax: zero.</p>

<h3>Example 2: Basic 50%, PF capped at the ₹25,000 wage ceiling</h3>
<p>Many employers cut PF only on the statutory wage ceiling instead of your full basic. The Union Cabinet raised that ceiling from ₹15,000 to <b>₹25,000 a month from 17 September 2026</b>. So PF becomes 12% of ₹25,000 = ₹3,000 a month from you and ₹3,000 from your employer.</p>

<p>That's ₹4,000 a month more in hand than Example 1, but ₹48,000 a year less going into PF. Older articles that still use the ₹15,000 ceiling show about ₹77,500 here, which is now out of date.</p>

<h3>Example 3: 10% of CTC is variable pay</h3>
<p>If ₹1,00,000 of your CTC is a yearly performance bonus, your fixed pay is ₹9,00,000. Basic at 50% of fixed pay is ₹4,50,000, so PF is ₹4,500 a month and gratuity is ₹21,645 a year.</p>
<p>Monthly gross becomes ₹68,696, and after PF and professional tax, <b>in-hand is about ₹63,996 a month</b>. The bonus comes once a year, if you hit your targets. Tax is still zero even with the full bonus (taxable income ₹8,49,355). So in the first year, budget on ₹64,000, not ₹71,000.</p>

<h3>Example 4: Basic 40% (older structure)</h3>
<p>If your company hasn't moved to the 50% structure yet, basic is ₹4,00,000. PF falls to ₹4,000 a month and gratuity to ₹19,240 a year. <b>In-hand: ₹73,530 a month</b>, about ₹2,400 more than Example 1, but with ₹24,000 a year less going into PF.</p>

<h2 id="labour-code">What the new labour codes changed</h2>
<p>The four labour codes came into force on <b>21 November 2025</b>, replacing 29 older labour laws. For your salary, one rule matters most: the <b>50% wage rule</b>.</p>
<p>Under the Code on Wages, "wages" means basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. Items like HRA, conveyance, overtime, commission, statutory bonus and employer PF are excluded, but they can't be more than 50% of total pay. If they are, the extra is added back to wages for PF and gratuity purposes. In practice, most companies have responded by setting basic at 50% of CTC.</p>
<p>What that means for you:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Slightly lower take-home.</b> At 10 LPA, going from 40% to 50% basic cuts in-hand by about ₹2,400 a month (Example 4 vs Example 1).</li>
<li><b>Bigger PF.</b> Your PF corpus grows faster, and PF interest is tax-free within the limits.</li>
<li><b>Bigger gratuity.</b> Gratuity is 15 days of last basic for each year of service, so a higher basic means a higher payout.</li>
<li><b>No change to tax at 10 LPA.</b> You're in the zero-tax band either way.</li>
</ul>
<p>The labour codes also extended gratuity to fixed-term employees, so contract roles are worth asking about too.</p>

<p>Two useful facts from this table. First, with this structure you pay zero tax up to a CTC of roughly <b>₹13.9 lakh</b>, because that's where taxable income crosses ₹12 lakh. Second, just above that line, marginal relief limits your tax to the amount by which taxable income exceeds ₹12 lakh. That's why ₹14 lakh only costs ₹7,623 in tax, while ₹15 lakh jumps to ₹77,832 once marginal relief no longer applies.</p>

<h2 id="old-regime">What if you pick the old regime at 10 LPA?</h2>
<p>Almost never worth it at this salary. Take Example 1 and claim the usual deductions in the old regime: ₹50,000 standard deduction, ₹2,400 professional tax, the full ₹1,50,000 under 80C (your ₹60,000 PF plus ₹90,000 in PPF or ELSS) and ₹25,000 for health insurance under 80D.</p>
<p>Taxable income: ₹9,15,950 − ₹50,000 − ₹2,400 − ₹1,50,000 − ₹25,000 = <b>₹6,88,550</b>. Old regime tax: 5% on ₹2.5 to 5 lakh (₹12,500) + 20% on ₹1,88,550 (₹37,710) = ₹50,210, plus 4% cess = <b>₹52,218</b>. The old regime's rebate only works up to ₹5 lakh of taxable income, so it doesn't help here.</p>
<p>Even if you also claim ₹1,20,000 of HRA exemption, tax is still about <b>₹27,258</b>. The new regime's zero wins easily. Run your own numbers in the <a href="../calculators/income-tax-calculator.html">Income Tax calculator</a>, which shows both regimes side by side, and the <a href="../calculators/hra-exemption-calculator.html">HRA calculator</a> if you pay rent.</p>

<h2 id="offer-letter">6 questions to ask before you sign a 10 LPA offer</h2>
<ol>
<li><b>What is the fixed pay and what is variable?</b> A ₹10 lakh CTC with ₹1.5 lakh variable is really an ₹8.5 lakh fixed offer.</li>
<li><b>Is PF cut on full basic or on the ₹25,000 ceiling?</b> This changes in-hand by up to ₹4,000 a month.</li>
<li><b>Is gratuity included in CTC?</b> If yes, remember you only get it after 5 years.</li>
<li><b>Is health insurance or a joining bonus inside CTC?</b> Insurance premiums and one-time bonuses inflate CTC without adding monthly cash. A joining bonus may also have a clawback if you leave early.</li>
<li><b>Which state will my payroll be in?</b> It decides professional tax.</li>
<li><b>Will TDS be cut under the new regime?</b> At 10 LPA it should be zero. Ask HR to confirm the regime in your first month so you don't get a surprise deduction.</li>
</ol>
