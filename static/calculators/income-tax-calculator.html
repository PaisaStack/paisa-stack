How this calculator works

India's new tax regime offers lower slab rates but almost no exemptions. The old regime keeps HRA, 80C, 80D and home loan interest deductions, but at higher rates. Which one wins depends entirely on your numbers. There's no universal answer, which is exactly why guessing costs people real money every year.

Enter three things and the calculator does the rest:

  • Gross annual income: your total salary or income for the year, before any deductions.
  • Salaried toggle: switches on the standard deduction, ₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old one.
  • Old-regime deductions: the total of everything only the old regime allows, such as 80C, 80D, home loan interest, NPS 80CCD(1B) and your HRA exemption.

It then works out taxable income and tax under both regimes, adds 4% health and education cess, and shows which regime is cheaper and by how much.

Income tax slabs for FY 2026-27

From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act, and the "previous year" and "assessment year" became a single tax year. The rates themselves did not change from last year.

Rebate: under the new regime, taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 pays zero tax (rebate up to ₹60,000). For a salaried person that means a salary of up to ₹12,75,000 is tax-free after the standard deduction. Under the old regime the rebate covers taxable income up to ₹5,00,000.

Marginal relief: if your new-regime taxable income is just above ₹12,00,000, your tax can't be more than the amount by which you crossed ₹12 lakh. At ₹12,25,000 taxable, slab tax would be ₹63,750, but you pay only ₹25,000 plus cess. The calculator applies this automatically.

4 worked examples

1. ₹12 lakh salary, ₹1.5 lakh in 80C

New regime: ₹12,00,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹11,25,000 taxable, fully covered by the rebate, so ₹0 tax. Old regime: ₹12,00,000 − ₹50,000 − ₹1,50,000 = ₹10,00,000 taxable, so ₹1,17,000 with cess. The new regime saves ₹1,17,000. Your PPF or ELSS investment is still worth making for its own sake, but it doesn't save tax here.

2. ₹18 lakh salary with a home loan

Deductions: 80C ₹1,50,000, home loan interest ₹2,00,000, 80D ₹25,000, a total of ₹3,75,000. Old regime: ₹13,75,000 taxable, so ₹2,34,000 tax. New regime: ₹17,25,000 taxable, so ₹1,50,800. The new regime is still ₹83,200 cheaper.

3. ₹25 lakh salary, rent in a metro, home loan, parents' insurance

Deductions: HRA exemption ₹3,00,000, 80C ₹1,50,000, home loan interest ₹2,00,000, 80D ₹75,000 (₹25,000 self plus ₹50,000 for senior citizen parents), NPS 80CCD(1B) ₹50,000, a total of ₹8,75,000. Old regime: ₹15,75,000 taxable, so ₹2,96,400. New regime: ₹24,25,000 taxable, so ₹3,19,800. Here the old regime wins by ₹23,400. This is the profile where checking both regimes really pays.

4. ₹13 lakh salary, just over the line

New regime taxable income is ₹12,25,000. Without marginal relief the tax would be ₹66,300 with cess. With it, you pay ₹26,000. Many simple calculators miss this.

Old vs new regime: a quick rule of thumb

  • Salary up to ₹12.75 lakh: the new regime is almost always better, because tax is zero.
  • No home loan and modest rent: the new regime usually wins at every income level.
  • High rent in a metro, a home loan, 80C and 80D all used: run both. Once your old-regime deductions cross about ₹7.5 to 8 lakh, the old regime often starts to win at incomes above ₹20 lakh.

Salaried people can switch regimes every year when filing the return. But the old regime has to be chosen in a return filed by the due date. File late and you are taxed under the new regime, as our belated ITR guide explains.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Surcharge on income above ₹50 lakh.
  • Higher old-regime exemption limits for senior citizens (₹3 lakh at 60+, ₹5 lakh at 80+).

Frequently asked questions

Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free in FY 2026-27?

Yes, under the new regime. Taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 gets a full rebate. For salaried people, the ₹75,000 standard deduction stretches that to a ₹12,75,000 salary.

Which is better for me, old or new regime?

For most people earning under ₹12.75 lakh, or anyone without a home loan and high rent, the new regime. If your old-regime deductions add up to ₹7.5 lakh or more and your income is above ₹20 lakh, the old regime can win. Enter your real numbers above to see.

What should I enter in the deductions box?

The total of: 80C investments (max ₹1,50,000), 80D health insurance (₹25,000 for yourself, up to ₹50,000 more for senior citizen parents), home loan interest on a self-occupied house (max ₹2,00,000), NPS under 80CCD(1B) (max ₹50,000), and your HRA exemption.

Can I switch between regimes every year?

Salaried individuals with no business income can choose each year at the time of filing the return, as long as it is filed by the due date. People with business income can switch back to the new regime only once.

Does the new Income-tax Act 2025 change my tax?

Not the rates. The new Act renamed and renumbered sections and replaced "assessment year" with "tax year", but slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the ₹12 lakh rebate carry over for FY 2026-27.

Is cess included in the result?

Yes. The tax shown includes the 4% health and education cess.
