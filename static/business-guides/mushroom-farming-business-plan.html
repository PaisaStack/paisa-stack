Mushroom farming business plan: free guide

Oyster mushrooms grow on wheat or paddy straw in a single spare room, give the first harvest in about 3 weeks, and sell at ₹120 to ₹250 a kg. This guide shows the setup cost, cost per bag, a real profit example and the licences you need.

Short answer: A one-room, 200-bag oyster mushroom unit needs about ₹25,000 of setup and ₹40 per bag to run. Each bag gives around 1 kg over 45 to 60 days. At ₹120/kg wholesale that's roughly ₹11,800 profit per batch, more if you sell direct. Start small, expect losses in the first batch, and line up buyers before you scale.

Why oyster mushroom for a first business

  • Low setup: no cold room or AC needed for most of the year in most of India. Oyster grows at about 20 to 30°C with 70 to 90% humidity.
  • Cheap raw material: wheat or paddy straw, which farmers often burn.
  • Fast cycle: first harvest in 20 to 25 days, 3 to 4 harvests (flushes) per bag over 45 to 60 days.
  • Many ways to sell: fresh, dried, powder, pickle, or ready-to-grow kits.

Button mushroom sells in bigger volumes but needs controlled cooling and composting, so it costs more to start. Milky mushroom suits hot regions like Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. This guide uses oyster because it's the easiest first step.

With about 6 batches a year from one room, that's roughly ₹70,000 to ₹1 lakh a year, and the ₹24,500 setup is recovered in about two batches. It's a good side income at this size. To make it a full-time business, you need 1,000+ bags across several rooms or a larger shed.

Be realistic about the first batch. New growers commonly lose 20 to 40% of bags to green mould or other contamination. Fresh oyster mushrooms also last only 2 to 3 days, so unsold stock is lost unless you dry it. Roughly 8 to 10 kg of fresh oyster makes 1 kg dried, which sells in 100 g packs for much more per kg.

Step-by-step setup

  1. Get trained. Your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture university usually runs short mushroom courses.
  2. Prepare the room. Clean, disinfect, add racks, and fix a fogger and hygrometer.
  3. Pasteurise the straw. Chop, soak and treat it with hot water or a chemical method, then drain it to about 65 to 70% moisture.
  4. Fill the bags. Layer straw and spawn, tie the bags, and punch small holes.
  5. Spawn run (2 to 3 weeks). Keep the bags in the dark until they turn fully white.
  6. Fruiting. Cut slits, give light and fresh air, keep humidity high, and harvest every few days for 3 to 4 flushes.
  7. Sell the same day, or dry the surplus.

Licences and registrations

  • FSSAI: basic registration (₹100/year) for turnover up to ₹12 lakh, a state licence above that. You need it to sell packed, dried or processed mushroom products.
  • Udyam (MSME): free, online, and needed for most subsidies and bank loans.
  • GST: fresh mushrooms are exempt. Dried mushrooms and powder are generally taxed, usually at 5%. Registration is needed once taxable sales cross the threshold or if you sell on marketplaces.
  • Trade licence from your local body, if your city requires one.

Subsidy and loans

  • PMEGP: 25 to 35% of project cost for a new unit, applied for online through KVIC with a project report.
  • Horticulture mission (MIDH/NHM): support for mushroom production units through your District Horticulture Officer.
  • PMFME: 35% support for drying, packing and processing units.
  • Mudra loan for working capital.

Rates and limits differ by state and change often. Subsidy is usually paid after the unit is set up, against a bank loan, so you need to fund the setup first. Planning a loan? The Loan EMI calculator shows your monthly payment, and the Goal Planner helps you save for the setup.

Where to sell

  • Local vegetable vendors, mandis, hotels, dhabas and cloud kitchens (wholesale, steady).
  • Direct to homes through WhatsApp groups and housing societies (higher price).
  • Dried mushroom and powder on Amazon, Flipkart or your own Instagram page (needs FSSAI and packing).
  • Ready-to-grow mushroom kits for gifting and schools.

Income from growing mushrooms in rooms is usually treated as business income, not tax-free agricultural income, so keep simple records from day one.

Frequently asked questions

How much investment is needed to start mushroom farming at home?

For a one-room oyster mushroom unit of about 200 bags, plan for roughly ₹25,000 of setup (racks, fogger, drums, hygrometer, tools) plus about ₹8,000 for the first batch of straw, spawn and bags. Exact costs vary by city and supplier.

How much profit can I make from 200 bags of oyster mushroom?

At about 1 kg per bag, 10% losses and ₹120/kg wholesale, a 200-bag batch earns about ₹21,600 against roughly ₹9,800 of costs, so close to ₹11,800 profit. Selling half directly at ₹180/kg lifts that to around ₹17,000. These are estimates, not guarantees.

Which mushroom is best for beginners in India?

Oyster mushroom (Pleurotus). It grows on wheat or paddy straw at normal room temperatures of about 20 to 30°C, needs no air-conditioning in most of India for much of the year, and gives its first harvest in about 3 to 4 weeks.

Do I need an FSSAI licence to sell mushrooms?

If you sell packed or processed mushrooms (dried, powder, pickles) you need FSSAI. A small business with turnover up to ₹12 lakh needs only basic FSSAI registration, which costs ₹100 a year. Above ₹12 lakh you need a state licence.

Is there GST on mushrooms?

Fresh mushrooms are treated as fresh vegetables, which are exempt from GST. Dried mushrooms and mushroom powder are generally taxed, usually at 5%. Confirm the HSN code and rate with a CA before you invoice.

Is there a government subsidy for mushroom farming?

Yes. Common routes are PMEGP (25 to 35% of project cost for a new unit), the horticulture mission (MIDH/NHM, through your District Horticulture Officer) and PMFME for drying and processing. Rates and limits vary by state and change often, and the subsidy is usually paid after setup against a bank loan.

How long does it take to get the first harvest?

About 20 to 25 days after spawning for oyster mushrooms. Each bag gives 3 to 4 flushes over roughly 45 to 60 days.

Costs, yields and prices are indicative 2026 estimates and vary by city, season, scale and supplier. Verify local quotes and current scheme rules before investing.
